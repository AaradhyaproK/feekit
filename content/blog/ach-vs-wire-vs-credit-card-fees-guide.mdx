Switching that invoice to **ACH Direct Debit** caps the total processing cost at **$5.00**, keeping $140.30 in your bank account on every transfer.

## 3. Rail 1: ACH Direct Debit (The High-Margin Workhorse)

The **Automated Clearing House (ACH)** network is the electronic payment backbone of the United States, administered by **Nacha**.

### How ACH Direct Debit Works
When your client pays via ACH Direct Debit, they authorize you to pull funds directly from their commercial checking account. Modern gateways like Stripe, Square, and QuickBooks utilize bank verification micro-deposits or instant API verification (via Plaid or Stripe Financial Connections).

### Fee Breakdown
* **Gateway Providers (Stripe, Gusto, Wave):** 0.80% per transaction, **strictly capped at $5.00**. Whether an invoice is $1,000 or $40,000, your processing fee never exceeds five dollars.
* **Direct Commercial Bank Accounts (Mercury, Brex, Chase Business):** Most modern fintech business accounts charge **$0.00 or $0.20 to $0.50 flat** per outgoing or incoming ACH transfer.

### Nacha SEC Codes & Dispute Windows
A vital legal distinction that protects B2B merchants:
1. **Consumer Accounts (PPD / WEB codes):** Consumers have **up to 60 calendar days** from statement date to dispute an unauthorized debit.
2. **Corporate Accounts (CCD code):** Commercial corporate accounts have only **2 business days** to return an unauthorized ACH debit under Nacha operating rules. Once two business days pass, corporate ACH transfers cannot be casually reversed.

## 4. Rail 2: Bank Wire Transfers (The High-Value Fortress)

A **wire transfer** is an electronic transfer of funds conducted directly between banks via real-time gross settlement systems like **Fedwire** (US Federal Reserve) or **CHIPS** (Clearing House Interbank Payments System).

### Cost Structure
Unlike ACH and credit cards, bank wire fees are typically fixed dollar amounts rather than percentages:
* **Incoming Domestic Wire Fee:** Your receiving bank charges between **$10.00 and $25.00** (many modern platforms like Mercury and Relay offer $0 incoming domestic wires).
* **Outgoing Wire Fee (Paid by Client):** The client's bank typically charges them **$20.00 to $35.00** to send a domestic wire.
* **International Wire (SWIFT):** Outgoing fees range from **$35 to $50**, with receiving intermediary bank fees ("landing fees") of $15 to $30.

### When Wire Transfers Are Essential
1. **Invoices Over $25,000:** When moving large sums of capital, a $15 flat wire fee represents less than 0.06% of the transaction volume.
2. **Zero Dispute Tolerance:** Wire transfers settle with **immediate, irrevocable finality**. Once the funds clear into your account, they cannot be retracted, bounced, or disputed.
3. **Same-Day Urgency:** When contracts or project milestones require proof of immediate cleared funds before work begins, domestic wires clear within hours.

## 5. Rail 3: Commercial Credit Cards (The Frictionless Tax)

Credit cards offer the lowest friction for clients: no bank routing numbers, no login portals, and instant payment confirmation. However, for B2B merchants, credit cards present two serious financial liabilities: **high uncapped percentages** and **long chargeback exposure**.

### The Corporate Interchange Penalty
Most B2B clients pay using corporate purchasing cards (P-Cards) or premium executive rewards cards (e.g., Amex Business Platinum, Chase Ink). These cards carry the **highest wholesale interchange rates** in the card network system—often ranging from **2.50% to 3.10% interchange alone** before processor markups.

If you accept credit cards on standard flat-rate pricing (2.9% + $0.30), your payment processor absorbs the interchange spread, but you still pay nearly 3% off your top line.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Level 2 and Level 3 Processing Data:** If you invoice enterprise clients via credit card, use a processor that supports Level 2/3 data (passing purchase order numbers, line items, and tax identifiers). This qualifies corporate card transactions for discounted interchange rates, saving **0.50% to 1.00%** per charge. ## 6. How to Build an Intelligent Payment Routing Strategy

You do not need to ban credit cards entirely to protect your profits. Instead, implement a tiered B2B fee routing policy:

### Rule 1: Set a Credit Card Threshold (e.g., $1,000)
In your Master Services Agreement (MSA) or engagement terms, state that invoices over a designated threshold must be settled via ACH direct debit or bank wire.
* Invoices **< $1,000**: Credit card, ACH, or debit card accepted.
* Invoices **≥ $1,000**: ACH Direct Debit or Wire Transfer required.

### Rule 2: Pass Credit Card Surcharges (Where Permitted)
If a client insists on using a corporate credit card to earn points on a $15,000 invoice, add a compliant credit card surcharge (typically 3.0%) to cover the merchant processing cost. In the US, credit card surcharging is legally permitted in most states as long as you disclose the fee prior to transaction completion.
*(Note: Surcharging debit cards is prohibited under federal law, and consumer card surcharging is illegal in the UK and EU).*

### Rule 3: Prominently Embed Bank Routing Details on Invoices
Clients often default to credit cards simply because it is easier than requesting your wire instructions. When creating invoices with our [Free Invoice Generator](/invoice-generator), include your:
* Bank Name
* Account Type (Business Checking)
* Routing Transit Number (ABA / ACH)
* Wire Routing Number (if different from ACH)
* Account Number

Providing these details directly in the invoice footer reduces payment delays and eliminates credit card processing deductions.

## 7. Summary: Which Payment Rail Should You Choose?

* **Use ACH Direct Debit if:** You invoice between **$500 and $25,000**, manage ongoing monthly retainer agreements, or want to cap your transaction fee at **$5.00** while keeping checkout frictionless for clients.
* **Use Bank Wire Transfers if:** The invoice exceeds **$25,000**, involves international clients, or requires guaranteed, irrevocable cleared funds before releasing deliverables.
* **Use Credit Cards if:** The transaction is under **$500**, represents an initial deposit for an emergency project, or the customer readily accepts an authorized processing surcharge.
