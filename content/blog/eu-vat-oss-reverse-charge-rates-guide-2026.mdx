## 2. How VAT Pricing Math Works: Adding vs. Extracting VAT

The single most common invoicing mistake occurs when extracting VAT from a **gross (tax-inclusive) receipt**. Multiplying a gross total by the headline VAT rate (e.g., `27%` or `19%`) always overstates the tax because the VAT rate applies to the **net price**, not the gross sum.

```
Adding VAT (Net → Gross):
  VAT Amount = Net Price × VAT Rate
  Gross Total = Net Price × (1 + VAT Rate)

Extracting VAT (Gross → Net):
  Net Price = Gross Total ÷ (1 + VAT Rate)
  VAT Amount = Gross Total − Net Price
```

### Worked Example A: Hungary 27% ÁFA (Net vs. Gross)
Suppose an agency bills a consumer in Budapest on a **€1,000.00 net** scope of work, or reconciles a **€1,270.00 gross** checkout payment:

- **Forward (Add 27% VAT to €1,000.00 net):**
  - `VAT = €1,000.00 × 0.27 = €270.00`
  - `Gross Invoice = €1,000.00 + €270.00 = €1,270.00`
- **Reverse (Extract 27% VAT from €1,270.00 gross):**
  - **Wrong method:** `€1,270.00 × 0.27 = €342.90` (overstates VAT by €72.90!)
  - **Correct statutory method:** `€1,270.00 ÷ 1.27 = €1,000.00` net, leaving **`€270.00` VAT** (an effective `21.26%` of the gross sum).

### Worked Example B: Germany 19% Mehrwertsteuer
On a **€595.00 gross** receipt inclusive of Germany's standard **19% MwSt.**:
- `Net Revenue = €595.00 ÷ 1.19 = €500.00`
- `VAT Payable to BZSt = €595.00 − €500.00 = €95.00`

## 3. B2C Cross-Border Sales: The €10,000 EU One-Stop Shop (OSS) Rule

If you sell consumer software, digital downloads, online courses, or physical goods shipped across EU borders to **non-VAT-registered individuals (B2C)**, place-of-supply rules tax the purchase in the **customer's country**, not yours.

### How the €10,000 Threshold Works
1. **EU-Based Sellers (Union OSS):**
   - If your total cross-border EU B2C sales of goods and TBE (telecommunications, broadcasting, and electronic) services stay **at or below €10,000** across all 26 other member states combined, you can charge your **own domestic VAT rate** and report it on your home VAT return.
   - The moment cross-border EU B2C sales exceed **€10,000 in a calendar year**, you must charge the **buyer's local VAT rate** (e.g., 27% for Hungary, 19% for Germany, 20% for France) on every subsequent cross-border order.
2. **Non-EU Sellers (US, UK, Canadian SaaS & Agencies — Non-Union OSS):**
   - The €10,000 threshold **does not apply** to businesses established outside the EU.
   - Non-EU merchants selling digital services directly to EU consumers must charge destination VAT **from their very first €1 sale**.
   - Registering once under the **Non-Union OSS** in a single EU member state (such as Ireland for English portals) lets you collect destination VAT for all 27 EU countries and file one consolidated quarterly OSS return instead of holding 27 separate VAT registrations.

## 4. B2B Cross-Border Sales: Article 196 Reverse Charge & VIES Verification

Business-to-business (**B2B**) cross-border invoicing inside the EU works completely differently from B2C sales.

Under **Article 196 of the EU VAT Directive**, when you supply services to a VAT-registered business established in another EU member state, you do **not** charge VAT on the invoice. Instead, the tax obligation reverses onto your client.

### 4-Step B2B Reverse Charge Checklist
1. **Collect the Client's VAT ID:** Obtain the customer's EU VAT registration number before issuing the invoice (for example `DE123456789` for Germany or `FRXX999999999` for France).
2. **Verify on VIES Before Invoicing:** Run the VAT ID through the European Commission's official **VIES (VAT Information Exchange System)** database and save a timestamped PDF or consultation number as audit proof. Without a valid VIES check, tax authorities can reclassify the sale as B2C and bill you for the missing VAT.
3. **Charge 0% VAT with Mandatory Wording:** Set VAT to `0%` and print **"Reverse Charge — Article 196 EU VAT Directive (VAT to be accounted for by the recipient)"** along with both your VAT ID and the client's VAT ID.
4. **Report on Your EC Sales List:** Log the net B2B supply on your domestic VAT return and **EC Sales List** (Zusammenfassende Meldung in Germany, DES in France, or equivalent national filing).

## 5. Physical Goods Imports & IOSS (€150 Threshold)

If you ship low-value physical e-commerce parcels directly from outside the EU (such as the US, UK, or China) to EU consumers:

- **Consignments ≤ €150:** The **Import One-Stop Shop (IOSS)** lets you collect the customer's local VAT at checkout (e.g., 21% in Spain or 23% in Ireland) so the parcel bypasses import VAT collection delays at customs.
- **Consignments > €150:** IOSS cannot be used; normal customs clearance, import VAT, and applicable customs duties apply at the border (either Delivery Duty Paid via courier or billed to the customer upon arrival).

## Summary: Which EU VAT Workflow Applies to You?

- **Selling B2B to an EU company with a valid VIES VAT number:** Issue a `0%` Reverse Charge invoice referencing Article 196.
- **Selling B2C digital services from outside the EU:** Register for **Non-Union OSS** (zero threshold) or route checkout through a Merchant of Record.
- **Selling B2C from inside the EU:** Monitor the **€10,000 EU-wide threshold**; once crossed, enroll in **Union OSS** and bill each country's destination rate.
