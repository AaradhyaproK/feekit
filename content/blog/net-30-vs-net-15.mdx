Cash flow is the lifeblood of small businesses and independent service providers. More businesses fail from poor cash flow timing than from lack of gross profitability.

When you finish a project and email an invoice, the **payment terms** specified in your contract dictate when that revenue transforms into spendable liquidity. The two most widely utilized terms in B2B commerce are **Net 30** and **Net 15**.

In this guide from the **FeeKit Editorial Desk**, we examine the operational trade-offs between Net 30 and Net 15, how early payment discounts like 2/10 Net 30 work mathematically, and how to structure contracts to protect your working capital.

## What Do Payment Terms Mean?

In business invoicing, the prefix **"Net"** indicates that the full amount specified on the invoice is due after any eligible deductions or early discounts are applied. The number represents the count of **calendar days** the customer has to remit payment:

- **Due Upon Receipt:** Payment is expected immediately (within 24 to 48 hours).
- **Net 10:** Payment is due within 10 calendar days.
- **Net 15:** Payment is due within 15 calendar days (roughly two business weeks).
- **Net 30:** Payment is due within 30 calendar days (standard monthly billing cycle).
- **Net 60 / Net 90:** Extended commercial terms (common in retail, wholesale, and Fortune 500 corporate procurement).

## Net 15 vs Net 30: Detailed Comparison

| Evaluation Factor | Net 15 Terms | Net 30 Terms |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cash Flow Velocity** | Fast; minimizes working capital lag | Slower; cash tied up for a full month |
| **Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)** | Typically 15 to 22 actual days | Typically 30 to 45 actual days |
| **Industry Acceptance** | Common for solo freelancers, consultants, small studios | Standard corporate baseline for mid-market and enterprise |
| **Client Administration** | Requires responsive accounts payable | Matches bi-weekly or monthly corporate check runs |
| **Best Used For** | New clients, small projects, tight working capital | Established retainers, enterprise clients, large contracts |

### The Real-World Impact: Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)
In commercial operations, invoices are rarely paid on the exact day they are due. The average B2B invoice is paid **5 to 15 days past its stated terms**:
- An invoice with **Net 15** terms is typically paid around **Day 18 to 22**.
- An invoice with **Net 30** terms is typically paid around **Day 35 to 45**.

If your business has monthly software subscriptions, contractor payroll, or tax liabilities, operating on Net 30 terms means you are effectively financing your client&apos;s operations out of your own cash reserves for 45 days.

## The Mathematics of Early Payment Discounts: 2/10 Net 30

To accelerate cash collection without alienating corporate clients who mandate Net 30 terms, many businesses use **cash discount trade credit terms**, most commonly **2/10 Net 30**.

Under **2/10 Net 30**:
- If the client pays within **10 calendar days**, they deduct **2%** from the invoice total.
- If the client pays after day 10, they must pay the **100% full balance** within 30 days.

```
Example: $5,000 Invoice
- Paid on Day 8: Client pays $4,900 (Saves $100)
- Paid on Day 25: Client pays $5,000 full amount
```

### The Surprising Annualized Cost of 2/10 Net 30
```
Effective Annual Rate = (Discount % / (100 - Discount %)) × (365 / (Full Term - Discount Period))
Effective Annual Rate = (2 / 98) × (365 / (30 - 10)) = 0.0204 × 18.25 = 37.24%
```

Because skipping the 2% discount is financially equivalent to borrowing money at an astronomical **37.24% annual interest rate**, corporate finance teams with available cash will almost always prioritize paying 2/10 Net 30 invoices within the 10-day discount window!

## When Should a Small Business Use Net 15?

Net 15 terms are ideal under the following business conditions:

1. **Small-to-Medium Business Clients:** Small companies and startups have flexible accounts payable workflows and do not need 30 days to process an invoice.
2. **Weekly or Bi-Weekly Retainers:** If you provide continuous development or creative services, Net 15 prevents billing periods from overlapping.
3. **New Clients Without Credit History:** For the first two to three projects with a new client, Net 15 limits your credit exposure until trust is proven.
4. **Pass-Through Costs:** If your invoice includes out-of-pocket project expenses (e.g., ad spend, equipment, server costs), use Net 15 or Due Upon Receipt to avoid carrying debt.

## When Should a Business Accept Net 30?

Net 30 terms are appropriate and often necessary in these scenarios:

1. **Enterprise & Corporate Clients:** Large corporations have multi-tier approval chains (project manager approval → department head sign-off → accounting verification → bi-weekly batch disbursement). Demanding Net 15 from a Fortune 500 company can cause contract gridlock during procurement review.
2. **Long-Term High-Value Retainers:** For reliable clients with multi-year payment histories, Net 30 provides predictable monthly rhythm.
3. **To Win Competitive Bids:** When bidding on large corporate RFPs, offering standard Net 30 terms aligns with corporate vendor guidelines.

## How to Handle Late Payments and Contract Enforcement

Regardless of whether you choose Net 15 or Net 30, protect your accounts receivable with clear contractual rules:

### 1. The Statutory Late Fee Clause
Always state your late fee policy in your master agreement and restate it on the footer of every invoice:
> *"Terms: Net 30. Outstanding balances remaining unpaid after 30 days are subject to a late charge of 1.5% per month (18% annually) or the maximum statutory rate permitted by law."*

### 2. The Stop-Work Clause
Include a clause stating that all project deliverables, source files, and live hosting access will be paused if invoices remain unpaid past **15 days overdue**. Clients prioritize payments rapidly when ongoing work stops.

### 3. Systematic Reminder Automation
Establish a standard follow-up schedule:
- **7 Days Before Due Date:** Friendly automated reminder with invoice copy attached.
- **On the Due Date:** Courteous confirmation email.
- **7 Days Overdue:** Direct inquiry asking if there are processing questions.
- **15 Days Overdue:** Formal notification referencing the contractual stop-work clause.
