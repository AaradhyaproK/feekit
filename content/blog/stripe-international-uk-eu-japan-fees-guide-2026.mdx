Most founders assume Stripe charges **"2.9% + 30 cents"** everywhere in the world. In reality, Stripe's published pricing varies dramatically across the **United States, United Kingdom, Eurozone, Canada, Australia, Japan, and Singapore**—and card origin plus foreign exchange conversion can more than **double** your effective processing cost on cross-border orders.

A SaaS merchant incorporating in London pays **1.5% + 20p** on domestic UK cards, while a Tokyo digital store pays **3.6% with ¥0 fixed fee**, and a US merchant taking a converted European card pays **5.4% + $0.30** (`$5.70` on a `$100` checkout).

### Why Card-Origin Pricing Matters Most in the UK & Europe
Because UK and EU interchange regulations heavily cap fees on domestic consumer cards, Stripe UK's **1.5% + 20p** and Stripe Eurozone's **1.5% + €0.25** look half as expensive as US card processing.

However, the moment a US, Canadian, or Australian buyer enters their card on a UK or European checkout, the rate jumps to **3.25% + 20p** (UK) or **3.5% + €0.25** (Eurozone)—plus a **1.0% to 2.0% FX conversion surcharge** if converted across currencies. Global SaaS teams selling from London into New York often find their blended effective Stripe fee sits closer to **3.8%–4.5%**, not 1.5%.

## 2. Spotlight: Why Stripe Japan (3.6% + ¥0) Behaves Unlike Any Other Market

Among major economies, **Stripe Japan (`stripe.com/jp/pricing`)** stands out because it charges **3.6% with ¥0 fixed fee** on standard domestic cards.

In the US, UK, or Singapore, the fixed surcharge ($0.30, 20p, or S$0.50) turns small-ticket purchases into an effective fee trap:
- On a **$5.00 US checkout** (`2.9% + $0.30`), the fee is **$0.45** (**9.0% effective**).
- On a **S$10.00 Singapore checkout** (`3.4% + S$0.50`), the fee is **S$0.84** (**8.4% effective**).
- On a **¥1,000 Japan checkout** (`3.6% + ¥0`), the fee is **¥36** (**3.6% effective**).
- On a **¥100,000 Japan checkout** (`3.6% + ¥0`), the fee is **¥3,600** (**still 3.6% effective**).

For Japanese digital content platforms, creator micro-subscriptions, or low-AOV consumer apps, zero fixed fee protects unit margins cleanly at every order value. Model any JPY ticket size in the [Stripe Japan Fee Calculator](/tools/stripe-fee-calculator/japan).

## 3. How International Surcharges & FX Conversion Stack (US Account Example)

When a US business processes a `$100.00` order, compare the three cost tiers:

1. **US Domestic Card Settled in USD:**
   - Formula: `2.9% + $0.30 = $2.90 + $0.30 = $3.20`
   - **Net Payout:** **`$96.80`** (`3.20%` effective fee)
2. **Foreign-Issued Card Charged in USD (+1.5% Intl Surcharge):**
   - Formula: `2.9% + 1.5% + $0.30 = 4.4% + $0.30 = $4.70`
   - **Net Payout:** **`$95.30`** (`4.70%` effective fee)
3. **Foreign-Issued Card Charged in Local Currency & Converted to USD (+1.5% Intl + 1.0% FX):**
   - Formula: `2.9% + 1.5% + 1.0% + $0.30 = 5.4% + $0.30 = $5.70`
   - **Net Payout:** **`$94.30`** (`5.70%` effective fee)

> **Important Distinction:** Notice that the `+1.5%` international surcharge applies based on **where the customer's card was issued**, regardless of whether you bill in USD or EUR. The separate `+1.0%` (or `+2.0%` in UK/Canada) conversion fee applies whenever Stripe converts the transaction currency into your bank settlement currency.

## 4. Reverse Gross-Up Math for International Client Invoices

If you want to clear **exact net cash** on a cross-border invoice after Stripe takes its international and currency deductions, never add `4.4%` or `5.4%` on top of your net quote—because Stripe deducts its percentage from the **gross total**.

Apply the gross-up formula:

```
Gross Invoice = (Desired Net Payout + Fixed Fee) ÷ (1 − Total Percentage Rate)
```

### Worked Example: Netting Exactly $1,000.00 on a US Stripe Account
- **Domestic US Card (`2.9% + $0.30`):**
  - `Gross = ($1,000.00 + $0.30) ÷ (1 − 0.029) = $1,000.30 ÷ 0.971 = $1,030.18`
  - Verify deduction: `$1,030.18 × 2.9% + $0.30 = $29.88 + $0.30 = $30.18` → **$1,000.00 net**
- **International Card Settled in USD (`4.4% + $0.30`):**
  - `Gross = ($1,000.00 + $0.30) ÷ (1 − 0.044) = $1,000.30 ÷ 0.956 = $1,046.34`
  - Verify deduction: `$1,046.34 × 4.4% + $0.30 = $46.04 + $0.30 = $46.34` → **$1,000.00 net**
- **International Card With 1% FX Conversion (`5.4% + $0.30`):**
  - `Gross = ($1,000.00 + $0.30) ÷ (1 − 0.054) = $1,000.30 ÷ 0.946 = $1,057.40`
  - Verify deduction: `$1,057.40 × 5.4% + $0.30 = $57.10 + $0.30 = $57.40` → **$1,000.00 net**

## 5. How to Cut Cross-Border Processing & FX Costs Immediately

1. **Match Payout Accounts to Billing Currencies:** Add EUR, GBP, CAD, or AUD local receiving accounts (including multi-currency business accounts) in Stripe so foreign checkouts settle like-for-like without paying Stripe's 1.0%–2.0% conversion spread on every sale.
2. **Route High-Ticket B2B Invoices Off Cards:** For US B2B invoices above $500, Stripe **ACH Direct Debit** (`0.8%` capped at **`$5.00`**) cuts a $2,000 invoice fee from `$58.30` (or `$108.30` international) down to `$5.00`. In Europe, enable SEPA Direct Debit.
