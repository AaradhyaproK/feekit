## Core Domestic Pricing Comparison

For standard digital storefront sales conducted in United States Dollars (USD), the headline rates for standard domestic card transactions appear deceptively straightforward:

| Transaction Dimension | Stripe (Standard US) | PayPal (Standard Checkout) | Differential Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Standard Online Cards** | 2.9% + $0.30 | 3.49% + $0.49 | Stripe saves 0.59% + $0.19 |
| **In-Person (Card Present)** | 2.7% + $0.05 | 2.29% + $0.09 | Compare with [Square POS Calculator](/tools/square-fee-calculator) |
| **ACH Direct Debit** | 0.8% (capped at $5.00) | Not supported natively | Stripe saves on high-ticket B2B |
| **Manual Keyed-In** | 3.4% + $0.30 | 3.49% + $0.49 | Compare with [Authorize.Net Calculator](/tools/authorize-net-calculator) |
| **Chargeback Dispute Fee** | $15.00 | $20.00 | Stripe saves $5.00 per claim |

Notice the widening spread on standard ecommerce checkouts. If your average order value (AOV) is **$100.00**:
- **Stripe Net Deduction**: $100.00 × 0.029 + $0.30 = **$3.20** (Net payout: $96.80). Model this on the [Stripe Fee Calculator USA](/tools/stripe-fee-calculator/usa).
- **PayPal Net Deduction**: $100.00 × 0.0349 + $0.49 = **$3.98** (Net payout: $96.02). Model this on the [PayPal Fee Calculator USA](/tools/paypal-fee-calculator/usa).

On $1,000,000 in gross annual processing, routing card transactions through Stripe rather than standard PayPal Checkout delivers **$7,800.00 in direct bottom-line margin savings**. ## Cross-Border and FX Currency Conversion

International commerce introduces two distinct fee tiers that can erode gross margins if left unmodeled:
1. **International Card Surcharge**: Assessed whenever a customer uses a card issued outside the merchant's operating country.
2. **Currency Conversion Spread**: Assessed when settlement occurs in a different currency than the charge.

```
Total Cross-Border Fee = Base Processing Rate + International Surcharge (1.0% - 1.5%) + FX Spread (1.0% - 4.0%)
```

Stripe applies an extra **1.0%** for international credit and debit cards, plus an additional **1.0%** if currency conversion is required (total 2.0% markup). For United Kingdom merchants, card processing begins at 1.5% + 20p for domestic cards (see our [Stripe UK Calculator](/tools/stripe-fee-calculator/uk)).

In contrast, PayPal applies a **1.50%** international cross-border fee plus a **3.0% to 4.0%** retail foreign exchange spread above wholesale mid-market rates. For merchants with substantial foreign transaction volume, our [Wise vs Stripe FX Spread Comparison](/tools/wise-vs-stripe) illustrates how multi-currency settlement accounts dramatically curb FX losses.

## Micro-Transactions and Low-Ticket Economics

For businesses selling digital downloads, SaaS subscriptions, or gaming microtransactions priced under **$10.00**, standard percentage plus fixed fee pricing is punitive.

Consider a **$5.00 digital asset sale**:
- Stripe standard fee: ($5.00 × 2.9%) + $0.30 = **$0.445** (8.9% effective rate). Check with the [Stripe Fee Calculator USA](/tools/stripe-fee-calculator/usa).
- PayPal standard fee: ($5.00 × 3.49%) + $0.49 = **$0.665** (13.3% effective rate). Check with the [PayPal Fee Calculator USA](/tools/paypal-fee-calculator/usa).

Stripe provides custom micropayment arrangements (typically 5.0% + $0.10) for qualified digital goods accounts, reducing the fee on a $5.00 sale to $0.35 (7.0%). PayPal also provides a dedicated micropayment schedule (4.99% + $0.09 for domestic US).

## Effective Fee Rate by Ticket Size: Where the Gap Actually Opens

The headline spread of 0.59% + $0.19 is not a flat number in practice - it splits into a variable component that scales with volume and a fixed component that scales with transaction count. The table below isolates both parts at four common average order values:

| Average Order Value | Stripe Fee (2.9% + $0.30) | PayPal Fee (3.49% + $0.49) | Per-Transaction Gap | Stripe Effective Rate | PayPal Effective Rate |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$10.00** | $0.59 | $0.84 | $0.25 | 5.90% | 8.39% |
| **$50.00** | $1.75 | $2.24 | $0.49 | 3.50% | 4.47% |
| **$100.00** | $3.20 | $3.98 | $0.78 | 3.20% | 3.98% |
| **$500.00** | $14.80 | $17.94 | $3.14 | 2.96% | 3.59% |
| **$1,000.00** | $29.30 | $35.39 | $6.09 | 2.93% | 3.54% |

Two patterns matter here. First, **the absolute gap widens with ticket size**: a single $1,000 PayPal order costs **$35.39** in fees, more than twenty-five $10.00 Stripe orders cost (**$14.75** combined). Second, **the effective rate gap narrows as tickets grow**: on a $1,000 invoice the two gateways are only 61 basis points apart, while on a $10 order they are 249 basis points apart. A high-volume, low-ticket business (marketplaces, app stores, digital downloads) therefore faces a proportionally much larger penalty on PayPal than a low-volume, high-ticket business does. ## B2B Invoices: Why ACH Direct Debit Dominates Above $625

For service businesses, agencies, and B2B sellers, invoice size is usually the single biggest controllable fee variable - and it is the one most merchants never model.

Stripe supports **ACH Direct Debit** at **0.80%**, strictly capped at a **$5.00 maximum fee** with no fixed cent surcharge. PayPal does not offer a native ACH Direct Debit product at all: a PayPal invoice paid by a customer is processed on the 3.49% + $0.49 commercial schedule, so the 0.80% ACH rate is unavailable no matter how large the invoice is.

On a **$2,500 B2B invoice**:

- **Stripe ACH Direct Debit**: $2,500.00 x 0.008 = **$5.00** (capped) - net payout **$2,495.00**
- **Stripe card (2.9% + $0.30)**: $2,500.00 x 0.029 + $0.30 = **$72.80** - net payout **$2,427.20**
- **PayPal invoice (3.49% + $0.49)**: $2,500.00 x 0.0349 + $0.49 = **$87.74** - net payout **$2,412.26**

The ACH fee reaches its $5.00 ceiling at exactly **$625.00** ($625.00 x 0.008 = $5.00). Below that threshold, cards still win on convenience; above it, ACH is dramatically cheaper - **$67.80 cheaper than a Stripe card on a $2,500 invoice**. Route your large invoices through bank transfer and small ones through the card rail, and the average fee collapses. ## Refunds: The Fee You Never Get Back

Both gateways retain the original processing fee when a merchant issues a refund, and this is the single most-missed line item in ecommerce unit economics.

Take a **$500.00** order:
- Stripe debits **$14.80** on the sale and deposits $485.20.
- If the customer cancels and you refund $500.00, Stripe deducts the full $500.00 from your balance and **keeps the $14.80 fee**.
- On PayPal, the equivalent $500.00 checkout costs **$17.94** and PayPal **retains that fee** on a full refund.

For a fashion or apparel store running a 15% return rate, that retained fee is worth roughly 0.3% to 0.5% of gross revenue - money that never appears in a gross-margin calculation but comes straight out of net profit. ## Payout Timing and the Cost of Same-Day Cash

Processing rate is only half of the cost of a gateway; **when the money lands** is the other half.

- **Stripe**: standard US payouts run on a rolling **2-business-day** schedule. Eligible accounts can pull funds forward with Instant Payouts to a debit card for a percentage surcharge with a $0.50 minimum.
- **PayPal**: standard transfer from PayPal balance to a linked bank account typically settles in **1 to 3 business days**. An instant card transfer is available for a **1.75% surcharge** (minimum $0.25, capped at $25.00 per transfer).

The commercial consequence: a business paying contractors weekly in cash cannot afford to wait for a standard settlement cycle, and both gateways price that impatience. On a **$10,000** instant transfer, a 1.75% surcharge costs **$175.00** for same-day access to money you have already paid fees on. If you can bridge a two-day gap with a business credit line instead, the effective saving is the entire surcharge. ## Chargebacks and Merchant Dispute Protection

A lost chargeback incurs both the loss of product/revenue and an administrative fee:
- **Stripe**: Charges a flat **$15.00** dispute fee. If you submit counter-evidence and win the dispute, Stripe refunds the $15.00 fee in full.
- **PayPal**: Charges a flat **$20.00** fee ($15.00 standard, or £14 in the UK). If the transaction qualified under PayPal Seller Protection, the dispute fee may be waived.

For every 100 disputes you win or lose, that fee line is **$1,500 (Stripe) or $2,000 (PayPal)** - before a single dollar of principal is counted. The full rules, win-rate thresholds, and Visa dispute-monitoring ratios are broken down in the [Chargeback & Dispute Fees Guide](/blog/chargeback-and-dispute-fees-guide-2026).

## Final Verdict & Architecture Recommendation

For engineering-driven teams and modern web applications, **Stripe** provides an unmatched developer API, lower standard domestic fees, superior recurring billing support, and transparent ACH integration.

However, offering **PayPal as a secondary checkout button** alongside Stripe card inputs consistently lifts checkout conversion by **8% to 15%**, particularly for European and mobile shoppers who prefer not entering raw card credentials.
