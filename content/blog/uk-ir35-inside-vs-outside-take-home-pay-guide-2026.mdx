## 1. The Statutory Rules: Chapter 8 vs. Chapter 10 (ITEPA 2003)

IR35—officially the **Off-Payroll Working Rules** under Chapters 8 and 10 of the *Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003 (ITEPA)*—prevents disguised employment through an intermediary such as a Personal Services Company (**PSC**).

Who carries the legal risk of determining your status depends entirely on the size and sector of the end client:

- **Medium & Large Private Sector Clients + All Public Sector (Chapter 10):**
  - The **end client** holds the legal duty to determine IR35 status and issue a formal **Status Determination Statement (SDS)** explaining its decision.
  - If the client fails to take "reasonable care," the PAYE/NIC tax liability stays with the client rather than transferring down the chain to the agency.
- **Small Private Sector Clients (Chapter 8 Exemption):**
  - If your end client meets at least **two** Companies Act 2006 small-company criteria (**annual turnover ≤ £10.2 million**, **balance sheet total ≤ £5.1 million**, **≤ 50 employees**), the off-payroll reform does not apply.
  - Your own limited company remains responsible for assessing IR35 status under Chapter 8.

### HMRC's Three Primary Status Pillars
HMRC and tax tribunals look past contract labels and evaluate real working practices:
1. **Right of Substitution:** Does your company hold a genuine, unfettered right to send an equally qualified substitute to complete the deliverables at your own expense?
2. **Control:** Does the client dictate *what* outcome you deliver, or do they supervise *how, when, and where* you perform daily tasks like a line-managed employee?
3. **Mutuality of Obligation (MOO):** Does the engagement terminate cleanly upon project delivery without any contractual obligation for the client to keep offering rolling work or for you to accept it?

## 2. Inside IR35 vs. Outside IR35: The Complete Tax Waterfall

When an assignment falls **Inside IR35** (typically paid via an umbrella company or agency deemed-employment payroll), employment taxes are carved out of the assignment rate before personal tax applies:

| Tax Layer | Inside IR35 (Umbrella / PAYE) | Outside IR35 (UK Limited Company) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Allowable Business Expenses** | Disallowed against day rate (save limited reimbursed client travel/subsistence) | **Deducted pre-tax** (accountancy, insurance, hardware, software, pension) |
| **Employer Class 1 NICs** | **15.0%** above £5,000/yr secondary threshold (deducted from assignment rate) | **£0** on director salary set at £12,570 if Employment Allowance applies / low threshold impact vs £121k |
| **Apprenticeship Levy** | **0.50%** of gross payroll (where applicable on umbrella payroll) | **£0** |
| **Employee Class 1 NICs** | **8.0%** (£12,570–£50,270) + **2.0%** above £50,270 | **£0** on £12,570 optimal salary |
| **Income / Corporation Tax** | **PAYE**: 20% basic, 40% higher (>£50,270), 45% additional (>£125,140) | **Corporation Tax**: 19% (≤£50k profit), marginal taper up to 25% (≥£250k) |
| **Dividend Tax (Post-Corp Tax)** | N/A | **8.75%** basic, **33.75%** higher, **39.35%** additional (£500 tax-free allowance) |
| **Typical Gross Retention** | **~54% to 58%** | **~71% to 78%** |

> **Why the £100,000–£125,140 Band Hurts Inside IR35:** Under PAYE, every £2 of taxable salary above **£100,000** strips away **£1 of your £12,570 Personal Allowance**, creating an effective **60% income tax + 2% Employee NIC = 62% marginal tax trap** unless diverted via salary-sacrifice pension contributions. Outside IR35, retaining surplus profit inside the limited company avoids crossing £100,000 personal income until you choose to draw dividends.

## 3. Worked Comparison: £550/Day Across 220 Working Days (£121,000 Billing)

Consider a senior UK technology contractor working **220 billable days** at **£550.00/day** (**£121,000.00** annual gross billing) with **£3,000** in normal operating costs (accountant, PI insurance, software, equipment).

### Scenario A: Inside IR35 (Umbrella Assignment Rate Model)
1. **Gross Assignment Revenue:** `220 × £550.00 = £121,000.00`
2. **Umbrella Margin + Employer NI (15.0%) + Levy (0.5%):** `~£18,250.00` deducted from the assignment pool
3. **Gross Taxable PAYE Pay:** `£121,000.00 − £18,250.00 = £102,750.00`
4. **Employee Class 1 NI + PAYE Income Tax:** `~£33,950.00`
5. **Net Take-Home Cash:** **`£68,800.00`** (**`56.9%` net retention**)

### Scenario B: Outside IR35 (Limited Company + Optimal Salary & Dividends)
1. **Gross Limited Company Revenue:** `£121,000.00`
2. **Deduct Allowable Expenses:** `−£3,000.00` (leaves `£118,000.00` pre-salary profit; less `£12,570` director salary = `£105,430` profit before Corp Tax)
3. **Corporation Tax (19%–25% Marginal Band):** `~£22,450.00`
4. **Personal Dividend & Income Tax:** `~£12,350.00`
5. **Net Take-Home Cash:** **`£86,200.00`** (**`71.2%` net retention**)

### The Annual Penalty of Inside IR35
- **Inside IR35 Net Cash:** `£68,800.00`
- **Outside IR35 Net Cash:** `£86,200.00`
- **Net Annual Loss:** **`£17,400.00`** (`£1,450/month` less disposable income for the exact same client work!)

## 4. How to Negotiate: Required Inside IR35 Day Rate Formula

Never accept a 1-for-1 day rate when a client switches an existing role from Outside IR35 to Inside IR35. Use the target-net equivalence formula before quoting:

```
Required Inside Annual Gross = Target Outside Net Cash ÷ Expected Inside Retention Ratio
Required Inside Day Rate = Required Inside Annual Gross ÷ Annual Billable Days
```

Suppose you currently earn **£500/day Outside IR35** across 220 days (`£110,000` gross), retaining approximately **£78,000 net** after taxes and expenses:

1. At an average Inside IR35 retention ratio of **56.5%**:
   - `Required Inside Gross = £78,000 ÷ 0.565 = £138,053.10`
2. Divide across `220` working days:
   - `Required Inside Day Rate = £138,053.10 ÷ 220 = £627.51/day`
3. Percentage rate premium needed:
   - `(£627.51 − £500.00) ÷ £500.00 = +25.5%`

In other words, a **£500/day Outside IR35** contract delivers the same cash in your pocket as a **~£628/day Inside IR35** umbrella contract. (And on a **£550/day Outside** rate, you need roughly **£690/day Inside** to stand still.)

## 5. Practical Ways to Reduce Tax Leakage Inside & Outside IR35

- **Use Salary-Sacrifice Pension Contributions (Inside IR35):** Redirecting earnings above £100,000 (or £50,270) directly into a SIPP through an umbrella salary-sacrifice arrangement saves Employer NI, Employee NI, and 40%–60% marginal income tax.
- **Avoid Over-Retaining or Missing VAT Registration:** Remember that IR35 status applies to *income tax and NICs*, **not VAT**.
