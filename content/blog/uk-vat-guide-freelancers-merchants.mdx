## The £90,000 Rolling Turnover Rule

A frequent misconception among new entrepreneurs is assuming the registration threshold applies to a fixed accounting year (such as April to April). Under HMRC statutory guidance, the **£90,000 threshold applies to any rolling 12-month window**:

```
Rolling 12-Month Taxable Turnover = Sum of all UK sales (excluding exempt items) over the past 365 days
```

If your cumulative taxable turnover exceeds £90,000 at the end of any calendar month, you must notify HMRC within **30 days** of the end of that month. Your effective registration date begins on the first day of the second month following the breach.

## Core UK VAT Rates Breakdown

HMRC recognizes three primary tax schedules for taxable supplies:

| Schedule | Rate | Typical Qualifying Goods and Services |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Standard Rate** | 20% | Most retail merchandise, digital software, legal services, merchant gateway fees |
| **Reduced Rate** | 5% | Domestic electricity & gas, mobility aids, smoking cessation products |
| **Zero-Rated** | 0% | Most grocery food, children's clothing, physical & digital books, public transport |
| **Exempt** | N/A | Financial loans, insurance underwriting, postal services, health clinic treatments |

## Making Tax Digital (MTD) Digital Links

Under HMRC MTD regulations, all registered entities must maintain digital business records and file VAT returns using MTD-compatible software via the HMRC API.

Key compliance requirements:
- **No manual copy-pasting**: Data transfers between spreadsheets or accounting engines must be preserved through **digital links** (e.g., automated formulas, CSV imports, or direct API webhooks).
- **Audit trail retention**: Electronic VAT invoices and summary totals must be digitally preserved for a minimum of **6 years**.

## Calculating Input vs Output VAT

When filing quarterly returns, your net remittance to HMRC is calculated as:

```
Net VAT Payable = Output VAT (Charged to Customers) - Input VAT (Paid on Purchases)
```

If Input VAT exceeds Output VAT (common during early capital expenditure or inventory acquisition), HMRC refunds the difference directly to your nominated business bank account.
