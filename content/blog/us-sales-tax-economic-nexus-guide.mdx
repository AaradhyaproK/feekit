Ever since the Supreme Court's landmark 2018 ruling in *South Dakota v. Wayfair, Inc.*, remote online retailers, direct-to-consumer (DTC) brands, and digital SaaS providers must collect and remit state sales tax once their revenue or order volume crosses statutory benchmarks known as **Economic Nexus**.

![US Sales Tax Economic Nexus 50-State Compliance Map 2026](/blog/images/us-sales-tax-nexus.webp)

Prior to *Wayfair*, a state could only require a merchant to collect sales tax if the seller maintained a "physical presence" (such as a retail shop, executive office, employee, or leased warehouse) inside state borders under the 1992 *Quill Corp. v. North Dakota* standard. *Wayfair* overturned *Quill*, ruling that an out-of-state vendor's extensive virtual and economic contact with a state's consumer market creates sufficient constitutional nexus.

In **2026**, state departments of revenue have continued fine-tuning their enforcement guidelines, with over 15 states officially eliminating transaction-count thresholds (such as 200 individual orders) to focus exclusively on gross or retail sales volume. ## Physical Nexus vs Economic Nexus: The Two Triggers

Online merchants often mistakenly believe that compliance begins only when they cross economic sales thresholds. However, **physical nexus always takes precedence over economic nexus**:

1. **Physical Nexus Triggers**: Maintaining an office, retail store, home office of a remote employee, or travelling sales representative inside a state immediately creates physical tax nexus from dollar one.
2. **Third-Party Logistics & Amazon FBA Inventory**: Storing inventory inside an Amazon FBA fulfillment center or third-party logistics (3PL) warehouse creates automatic physical presence nexus in that state. If Amazon moves your inventory to an FBA warehouse in Tennessee or Pennsylvania, you have immediate physical nexus in that state regardless of sales volume.
3. **Economic Nexus Triggers**: Established purely by the volume of remote sales shipped into a state, even if you have zero physical property, employees, or inventory in that jurisdiction.

## The Standard Economic Nexus Benchmarks

While rules vary across all 45 states that levy general sales taxes, the majority of states follow one of three architectural models:

| Category | Typical Revenue Threshold | Transaction Count Rule | Prominent Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **High Revenue-Only Tier** | **$500,000** gross or retail sales | None (Transaction count eliminated) | California (AB 147), Texas (HB 2153) |
| **Standard Revenue-Only Tier** | **$100,000** gross sales | None (Transaction count eliminated) | Florida (SB 50), Washington, Colorado, Pennsylvania |
| **Dual Rule (Revenue OR Orders)** | **$100,000** OR **200 transactions** | Whichever threshold occurs first | Georgia, Michigan, North Carolina, New Jersey, Ohio |
| **Dual Rule (Revenue AND Orders)** | **$500,000** AND **100 transactions** | Both conditions must be met | New York (N.Y. Tax Law § 1101(b)(8)) |

## State Deep Dives: Major Commercial Hubs

### 1. California Economic Nexus ($500,000 Gross Sales)
California enforces a clean **$500,000 gross sales threshold** delivered into the state during the preceding or current calendar year under Assembly Bill 147 (Cal. Rev. & Tax. Code § 6203). California completely eliminated transaction count tests, protecting small artisans with thousands of sub-$10 orders. 

Remote sellers who cross $500,000 must register with the California Department of Tax and Fee Administration (CDTFA) and collect base state sales tax (7.25%) plus applicable local district transactions and use taxes ranging up to 10.75% (such as Los Angeles at 9.50% or Hayward at 10.75%).

Audit local municipal surtaxes across California cities with our [California Sales Tax Calculator](/tools/sales-tax-calculator/california).

### 2. Texas Single Local Tax Option ($500,000 Total Revenue)
Texas utilizes a **$500,000 total revenue threshold** from sales of tangible personal property or services into Texas during the prior 12 calendar months (Tex. Tax Code § 151.051). 

To relieve remote sellers of tracking over 1,500 distinct city, county, and transit taxing jurisdictions, Texas offers an innovative alternative: remote sellers can elect to collect a flat **Single Local Tax (SLT) rate of 1.75%** on top of the 6.25% state rate (totaling a flat 8.00% statewide collection rate), rather than tracking street-level municipal rates.

Audit local Texas rates with our [Texas Sales Tax Calculator](/tools/sales-tax-calculator/texas).

### 3. New York Multi-Criteria Nexus ($500,000 AND 100 Transactions)
New York enforces a dual-condition test: sales of tangible personal property delivered into the state must exceed **$500,000 AND 100 separate transactions** in the immediately preceding four sales tax quarters (N.Y. Tax Law § 1101(b)(8)(iv)). 

A merchant with $600,000 in volume from only 40 large B2B commercial shipments does not trigger New York economic nexus. Conversely, a seller with 5,000 orders totaling $200,000 does not trigger nexus because revenue fell below $500,000.

Calculate exact Empire State borough and county tax with the [New York Sales Tax Calculator](/tools/sales-tax-calculator/new-york).

### 4. Florida Economic Nexus ($100,000 Remote Sales)
Florida enacted economic nexus under Senate Bill 50, establishing a **$100,000 remote retail sales threshold** of taxable tangible personal property delivered into the state during the previous calendar year. Florida abolished all transaction count tests. The statewide base rate is 6.00%, with county discretionary surtaxes adding up to 1.50% (capping at 7.50% in Hillsborough and Miami-Dade). 

Check county rates with our [Florida Sales Tax Calculator](/tools/sales-tax-calculator/florida).

### 5. Illinois Hybrid Destination Nexus ($100,000 or 200 Transactions)
Illinois enforces a standard **$100,000 sales threshold OR 200 separate transactions** for remote sellers under the Leveling the Playing Field for Illinois Retail Act. Remote sellers must collect the Destination Retailers' Occupation Tax (ROT), combining the 6.25% state rate with local municipal and Regional Transportation Authority (RTA) taxes that push combined rates up to 10.25% in Chicago.

## Origin vs Destination Sourcing: The Rules of Delivery

How sales tax is calculated depends directly on statutory sourcing rules:

- **Destination-Based Sourcing (The Majority Rule)**: Tax is calculated based on the delivery address of the buyer. If you ship from New Jersey to a customer in Los Angeles, you charge the composite Los Angeles rate. All interstate remote commerce is governed by destination sourcing.
- **Origin-Based Sourcing (Intrastate Exception)**: Applied by a minority of states (such as Texas, Ohio, Pennsylvania, and Virginia) for sales that originate and terminate within the same state. An in-state merchant calculates sales tax based on the location of their store or warehouse, rather than where the customer lives.

## Marketplace Facilitator Laws & Direct Store Thresholds

If you sell through platforms like Amazon, Shopify Markets, Etsy, or Walmart Marketplace, these platforms act as statutory **Marketplace Facilitators** in all 45 sales tax states. The platform calculates, collects, and remits sales tax on your behalf.

However, sales made through facilitators almost always **count toward your gross threshold** for direct sales on your proprietary website. 

For example, if you generate $95,000 in sales through Amazon FBA and $10,000 through your independent Shopify DTC store into Georgia (which has a $100,000 threshold), your combined $105,000 gross volume breaches Georgia economic nexus. You do not owe tax on the $95,000 (Amazon remitted it), but you are now legally required to register with the Georgia Department of Revenue and collect sales tax on the $10,000 of direct Shopify sales.

## The Resale Exemption Certificate Defense

To avoid collecting sales tax on wholesale transactions, you must obtain and archive a valid state-specific **Resale Exemption Certificate** (such as California Form CDTFA-230, Texas Form 01-339, or New York Form ST-120):

- **Certificate Requirements**: Must contain the buyer’s legal entity name, state tax ID number, description of items purchased, and authorized signature.
- **Statutory Retention Mandate**: Retain executed certificates on file for a minimum of **3 to 4 years** (6 years in New York).
- **The "Audit Risk" Rule**: In a state sales tax audit, any invoice lacking an executed exemption certificate on file is automatically classified as a taxable retail sale. The state auditor will assess the uncollected sales tax directly against your company, supplemented by statutory late penalties and daily compounding interest.
